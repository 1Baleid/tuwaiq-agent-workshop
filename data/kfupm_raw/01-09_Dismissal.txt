[UPDATED RULE] New academic regulation: undergraduate students are limited to a maximum of three
regular terms to complete the Freshman level, including any dropped, withdrawn, or no-show term.
Students who do not complete the Freshman level within three regular terms (including dropped,
withdrawn, or no-show terms) are subject to dismissal at the end of the third regular term. Source:
KFUPM Registrar announcement "New Academic Regulation: Restriction limiting undergraduate students
to a maximum of three regular terms to complete the Freshman level". A student is dismissed if he
receives three consecutive academic probations. Following the recommendation of the Deanship of
Admissions & Registration in coordination with the college council, the University Council may allow
the student a fourth opportunity to improve his cumulative GPA. A student is dismissed if he fails
to complete the graduation requirements within an additional period equal to one half of the
original program's duration. The University Council, based upon the recommendation of the Deanship
of Admissions & Registration in coordination with the college council, may exempt the student from
this restriction and give him the opportunity to complete the graduation requirements within an
additional period of maximum duration equal to that of the original program. A student is dismissed
if he fails to complete the graduation requirements within an additional period equal to that of the
original program's duration. Following the recommendation of the Deanship of Admissions &
Registration in coordination with the college council, the University Council may grant the student
an additional opportunity not exceeding two regular semesters to complete the graduation
requirements. The Deanship of Admissions & Registration informs the student of his dismissal and
cancels his enrollment. A dismissed student is obliged to obtain a clearance form from the Deanship
of Student Affairs and have it signed by all the relevant departments as mentioned in Article
Nineteen.
