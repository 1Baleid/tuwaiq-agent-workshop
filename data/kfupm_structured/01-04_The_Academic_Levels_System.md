# Undergraduate Regulations, Section 4: The Academic Levels System (updated with current rules)

## Programs organized by full academic year (Article Six)

According to the rules and regulations established by the University Council, some colleges may formulate their programs on the basis of a full academic year. In this case the academic year is equivalent to two academic levels.

## Semesters and levels (Article Seven)

The academic levels system divides the academic year into two regular semesters. There may be a summer semester, the duration of which is considered as half a regular semester. The degree requirements are divided into various levels in accordance with the degree plan approved by the University Council.

Implementation rule: for some of the University programs, a semester may be divided into two parts. The governing regulations shall be approved by the University Council.

## Registration rules and the academic advisor's approval (Article Eight, rule 1.1)

The University Council sets up the detailed regulations which govern registration, dropping, and adding of courses within the levels of the approved degree plan while ensuring the specified minimum course load for the students.

The approval of the academic advisor is required for completing the registration process in accordance with the rules set by the University.

## Early registration (Article Eight, rule 1.2)

At approximately the middle of the first (fall) semester, early registration is held for the courses to be taken by students during the second (spring) semester; and in the middle of the second semester, early registration is held for both the coming summer semester and the first semester of the following academic year. Early registration is required of all enrolled students during the semester. Students who early registered for a particular semester are also required to do registration confirmation on the scheduled registration day for that semester.

## Formal registration confirmation, no registration by proxy (Article Eight, rule 1.3)

Formal registration confirmation is held at the beginning of each semester or summer semester. Students are required to complete registration confirmation as specified in the academic calendar. Each student must do registration confirmation himself. Registration by proxy or any other way is not permitted at all.

## Late registration (Article Eight, rule 1.4)

If necessary, a student may be allowed to register late during the period specified in the academic calendar, in accordance with the rules set by the University. The student is responsible for all the consequences of his late registration.

## Adding and dropping courses: the first two weeks (Article Eight, rule 1.5)

A student may change his registration by adding some courses during the period specified in the academic calendar. Also, courses will not appear in the student's transcript if dropped during the first two weeks of classes in a regular semester (the first week in a summer semester). For dropping courses, see the Implementation Rules of Article 13.

## Conditions for adding a course (Article Eight, rule 1.5)

1. The course load should not exceed the maximum allowable limit (see the course load rules of this Article).
2. The courses added should not result in a conflict in the student's schedule or final examinations.
3. If a student desires to add a course section that is closed, and taking into consideration the evenness of distribution of students among sections of that course, then he must get the approval of the Chairman of department offering the course, and submit it to the Deanship of Admissions & Registration within the specified time.

## Auditing a course: who approves

A student can change the status of a course for which he has already registered, from regular to audit, with the concurrence of the course instructor and subsequent approval of the Chairman of the department offering the course, and the Chairman of the student's major department.

## Auditing a course: conditions

While making a request to audit a course, the student must bear in mind that:

1. he can audit a course only if he is expected to graduate in the current semester;
2. he cannot audit a course that he needs in order to graduate;
3. the "audit" status for a course cannot be changed to "credit" status after the "adding" period;
4. once a course has been audited, it cannot be repeated for credit in subsequent semester(s) except if it is a required course in a new major. This exception will require approval of the advisor, the Chairman of the (major) department, the Dean of the college and the Vice Rector for Academic Affairs;
5. the deadline for receiving audit requests by the Deanship of Admissions & Registration is the last day for dropping course(s) with the grade of W in the respective term as indicated in the academic calendar. (See Implementation Rules for Article 28.)

## Course substitution in the degree plan

Some courses can be exchanged or substituted by other courses with the approval of the relevant Vice Rector of the University, then informing the Deanship of Admissions & Registration for implementation. This is only possible in cases such as: if certain courses in the student's degree plan are discontinued, or changes are made in the contents of a course, or a new curriculum is adopted that does not include certain courses required by the student.

## Repeating a course (failed, D or D+)

A student who obtains a failing grade in a required course must repeat this course. Additionally, a student can repeat a course for which he previously obtained a D or D+ grade. The last grade will reflect the student's performance in such a course. Should a student repeat a required course in which he had earned a D or D+ grade, and fail, he must repeat the course and get a passing grade. All the grades are included in the GPA calculation in the student's transcript.

## Cooperative Program: what it is

Some students, according to the requirements of their majors and degree plans, should spend a period (not exceeding 28 weeks) of practical training in their major field. The student must remain in continuous contact with his academic department during the training period.

## Cooperative Program: eligibility (more than 85 credit hours)

In order to qualify for enrollment in the Cooperative Program the student should:

1. have completed more than 85 credit hours of his degree plan and should complete the cooperative assignment before his last semester at the University;
2. have completed all the required courses as identified by his major department;
3. have a cumulative GPA and major GPA of 2.00 or above;
4. not be discontinued from study;
5. not be allowed to take any other courses along with the Cooperative Program.

## Summer Training: what it is

Some students, according to the requirements of their majors and degree plans, should spend a summer training period of eight (8) weeks in their major field. The student should complete the summer training period before his last semester at the University.

## Summer Training: eligibility (more than 65 credit hours)

In order to qualify for enrollment in Summer Training the student should:

1. have completed more than 65 credit hours of his degree plan;
2. have completed all the required courses as identified by his major department;
3. have a cumulative GPA and major GPA of 2.00 or above;
4. not be discontinued from study;
5. not be allowed to take any other courses along with the Summer Training.

## Course load: definition

A course load is defined as the number of credit-hours for which a student is registered in a regular semester or a summer semester. The course load varies from one major to another and is determined by the rules below.

## Course load in good standing: minimum 13, maximum 19 credit hours

For a student with Good Standing in a regular semester: [UPDATED RULE] effective term 242, the minimum course load is 13 credit hours during a regular semester (previously 12). This condition is relaxed in the last semester before graduation for students left with fewer than 13 credit hours. Source: KFUPM Registrar announcement "Minimum load for undergraduate students in a regular term increased from 12 credits to 13 credits".

The maximum course load is 19 credit hours.

## Course load in good standing: 21 credit hours with GPA 3.00, and the summer limit

A student is permitted to register for 21 credit hours with the approval of his department Chairman, if the student has maintained a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.00 in the preceding semesters that include the last 28 credit hours taken by the student.

The maximum course load in a summer semester is 8 credit hours.

## Course load on Academic Warning or Probation: minimum 13, maximum 15 credit hours

[UPDATED RULE] Effective term 242, the minimum course load for a student on Academic Warning or Probation is 13 credit hours in a regular semester (previously 12).

The maximum course load is 15 credit hours in a regular semester, and 7 credit hours in a summer semester.

## Course load on Academic Warning or Probation: 16 to 19 credit hours with an e-petition

[UPDATED RULE] A student on Academic Warning or Probation may register 16 to 17 credit hours with an e-petition approved by the Department Chairman, or 18 to 19 credit hours with an e-petition approved by the Department Chairman and the College Dean. Source: KFUPM Registrar page "Registration Regulations".

## Course load in the last term before graduation: up to 20 or 21 credit hours

The maximum course load is 20 credit hours in a regular semester and 9 credit hours in a summer semester. The student should have maintained a minimum cumulative GPA of 2.00 in the preceding semesters that include the last 28 credit hours taken by the student.

[UPDATED RULE] A graduating student in good standing with a cumulative GPA above 2 may register 21 credit hours in a regular term and 9 credit hours in a summer term, with an e-petition approved by the Department Chairman. Source: KFUPM Registrar page "Registration Regulations".

## The transcript (academic record) and its release

At the end of each academic term, a copy of the student's academic record (the Transcript) is made available for him. No copy of the transcript is issued, given or sent to any outside agency or any other person without a written authorization by the student. No partial records are issued. The transcript must comprise the complete academic record of the student from the date of admission to the issue date.

The accuracy of a student record is of the utmost importance and errors should be brought to the immediate attention of the Deanship of Admissions & Registration.
