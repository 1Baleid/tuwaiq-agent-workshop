# Undergraduate Regulations, Section 11: Examinations and Grades (updated with current rules)

## Class work is at least 30% of the course score (Article Twenty-Two)

The class work score shall comprise not less than 30% of the course total score, as found by the college council on the basis of the recommendation of the department council offering the course.

## How class work is evaluated (Article Twenty-Three)

The class work score is evaluated either by:

(a) oral and practical examinations, research, other class activities or some or part of all these and at least one written examination; or,

(b) at least two written examinations.

## Practical or oral tests in the final examination (Article Twenty-Four)

Based upon the recommendation of the department council offering the course, the college council may include practical or oral tests in the final examination of any course, and allocate a percentage to these tests as part of the final examination score.

## Incomplete (IC) grade: what it means and when it becomes F (Article Twenty-Five)

Upon the instructor's recommendation, the council of the department which teaches the course may allow the student to complete the requirements of any course during the next term. In such an event, the grade IC will be recorded for the student in his academic record. IC grades are not included in the calculation of the semester and cumulative GPA until the student obtains his final grade in the course by completing all the requirements. If no change has been made in the IC grade after the lapse of one semester, the IC status will be changed to an F grade which will be included in the calculation of semester and cumulative GPA.

## Incomplete (IC) grade: when it is given and the instructor's report (Article Twenty-Five, rules 1 and 2)

1. The course instructor may allow the student to complete the course requirements during the following term if there are exceptional circumstances which are beyond the student's control.
2. The course instructor assigns an IC grade for the student and submits a report to the department Chairman indicating the reasons and justifications for assigning the IC grade, and identifies the work and the time required to complete the course requirements.

## Incomplete (IC) grade: deadline to complete, and co-op exceptions (Article Twenty-Five, rule 3)

The student must complete the course requirements by the end of the next regular semester. However, exceptions may be made in the following cases:

(a) A student who attained an IC grade in the co-op program may, with the approval of the department Chairman, extend completion of the course requirements for one additional regular semester.

(b) A student who attained an IC grade in a course in the semester preceding his co-op program may, with the approval of the department Chairman, extend completion of that course's requirements within a maximum period of one regular semester after returning from the co-op program.

## Incomplete (IC) grade: changing it to a grade or to F (Article Twenty-Five, rules 4 and 5)

When the student completes the course requirements within the specified period, the course instructor changes the student grade from IC to the new earned grade. The instructor also informs the Deanship of Admissions & Registration of the grade change within this period through the department Chairman concerned.

The Deanship of Admissions & Registration changes the grade to F and informs the student, course instructor and department Chairman accordingly if the grade has not been changed by the instructor within the specified period.

## Incomplete (IC) grade: effects on repeating, standing and prerequisites (Article Twenty-Five, rules 6 to 8)

- A student cannot repeat a course in which he previously earned an IC grade and the said grade has not been changed.
- If a student has an IC grade, this results in the suspension of the student's academic standing during that semester. This also includes the suspension of distinction status.
- A student is not allowed to register for a course wherein he earned an IC grade in the pre-requisite(s) of that course.

## Courses with other evaluation methods (Article Twenty-Six)

Courses involving symposia, research, field work, or of a practical nature, may be excluded from some or all the rules of Articles 22, 23 & 25 following a decision by the college council and the recommendation of the department council teaching the course. The college council identifies alternate ways to evaluate the student's achievement in such courses.

## In Progress (IP) grade for research courses (Article Twenty-Seven)

If any course of a research nature requires more than one semester for its completion, the student will be assigned an IP grade, and after the completion of the course, the student will be given the grade he has earned. However, if he fails to complete the course on time, the department council teaching the course may approve the assignment of an IC grade for this course in his record.

## Grade scale: percentages, letter grades and grade points (Article Twenty-Eight)

The grades a student earns in each course are calculated as follows (percentage, grade, code, points out of 5.00, points out of 4.00):

- 95 - 100: Exceptional, A+, 5.00, 4.00
- 90 - less than 95: Excellent, A, 4.75, 3.75
- 85 - less than 90: Superior, B+, 4.50, 3.50
- 80 - less than 85: Very Good, B, 4.00, 3.00
- 75 - less than 80: Above Average, C+, 3.50, 2.50
- 70 - less than 75: Good, C, 3.00, 2.00
- 65 - less than 70: High Pass, D+, 2.50, 1.50
- 60 - less than 65: Pass, D, 2.00, 1.00
- Less than 60: Fail, F, 1.00, 0.00

## How the final course grade is decided (Article Twenty-Eight, rule 1)

The student's final course grade will be one of the nine levels mentioned in the Article and his grades will be calculated in accordance with this distribution. The course instructor may consider other known assessment methods such as the grade average and the standard deviation in determining the student's end-of-course grade which reflects his achievement in the course.

## Audit (AU), pass/fail (NP, NF) and withdrawal (WP, WF) grades (Article Twenty-Eight, rules 2, 3 and 5)

The grade AU will be assigned to students who attend a course as auditors without being given any grades, regardless of their performance in the course. The effect of this assignment on the student's cumulative or semester grade is the same as the grade "No grade-Pass" or NP. However, if the instructor informs the Deanship of Admissions & Registration that the student was absent for more than one third of the classes, the course will be eliminated from his record. See Implementation Rules for Article 8.

The grades "No grade-Pass (NP)" or "No grade-Fail (NF)" are assigned for courses offered on the basis of pass or fail.

The grade "Withdrawn with Pass (WP)" or "Withdrawn with Fail (WF)" is given in accordance with Implementation Rules for Article 13.

## Cooperative Program in the summer: IP grade becomes NP or F (Article Twenty-Eight, rule 4)

If a student is registered in the Cooperative Program in summer semester and is assigned an IP grade in it, the IP grade will be changed to:

1. NP grade, if the student passes the Cooperative Program.
2. F grade, if the student fails the Cooperative Program.

## Graduation rank by cumulative GPA (Article Twenty-Nine)

In accordance with the requirements of Article 19, and based on the cumulative Grade Point Average achieved by a graduating student, his graduation rank is assigned to one of the following (cumulative GPA out of 5.00, and out of 4.00):

- Excellent: 4.50 - 5.00, or 3.50 - 4.00
- Very Good: 3.75 - less than 4.50, or 2.75 - less than 3.50
- Good: 2.75 - less than 3.75, or 1.75 - less than 2.75
- Pass: 2.00 - less than 2.75, or 1.00 - less than 1.75

## Graduation honors until Term 281: First and Second Honors (Article Thirty)

First honors will be granted to graduating students who achieve a cumulative GPA of 4.75 - 5.00 (out of 5.00) or 3.75 - 4.00 (out of 4.00). Second honors will be granted to graduating students who achieve a cumulative GPA of 4.25 - less than 4.75 (out of 5.00) or 3.25 - less than 3.75 (out of 4.00).

In order to be eligible for the first or the second honors the student:

(a) must not have failed in any course at the university he is currently attending or any other university;

(b) must have completed all graduation requirements within a period of duration ranging between the maximum and minimum limits for completing the program of study in a college;

(c) must have completed 60% or more of the graduation requirements at the university from which he graduates.

## Graduation honors until Term 281: Third Honors (Article Thirty, rules 1 and 2)

Third honors will be granted, at the time of graduation, to students who achieve a cumulative GPA of more than 3.00 (out of 4.00), and the conditions for offering first and second honors do not apply. However, they must fulfill the terms of paragraphs (b) and (c) of Article 30.

The provisions of (a) of Article 30 do not apply to a student who has failed in any Preparatory Year course.

[UPDATED RULE] Graduation honors are being revised. The current criteria for First, Second and Third Honors continue until the end of First Semester 2028-2029 (Term 281). Effective Second Semester 2028-2029 (Term 282), graduation honors will be: Highest Honors: cumulative GPA > 3.85; High Honors: cumulative GPA > 3.70; Honors: cumulative GPA > 3.50. ## Semester distinction list: old criteria (Article Thirty, rule 3)

At the end of each semester, the Deanship of Admissions & Registration records the names of distinguished students on the University distinction list, on the basis of their semester GPA and the quality points earned in this semester. These are the OLD distinction criteria (see the new criteria below):

- First Distinction: semester GPA 3.75 - 4.00, and 60 quality points or above
- Second Distinction: semester GPA 3.50 - 3.74, and 56 quality points or above
- Third Distinction: semester GPA 3.00 - 3.49, and 48 quality points or above

## Semester distinction: new criteria and who they apply to (updated rule)

[UPDATED RULE] Semester honors (distinction) have been revised and are being applied in phases. NEW criteria: First Distinction: semester GPA > 3.85 and quality points > 58; Second Distinction: semester GPA > 3.70 and quality points > 56; Third Distinction: semester GPA > 3.50 and quality points > 53.

Who the NEW criteria apply to: Preparatory students from Term 241; Freshmen from Term 251; Sophomores from Term 261 (current term); Juniors from Term 271; Seniors from Term 281. Until then, the OLD criteria still apply to Juniors (until Term 271) and Seniors (until Term 281).

## Rank of Excellent for a year, and distinction rewards (Article Thirty, rules 4 and 5)

A student earns the rank of 'Excellent' for an academic year if he achieves one of the distinction ranks in both the first and second semesters of that year.

A student receives his distinction reward remuneration in the semester in accordance with the Regulations for Financial Affairs in the Saudi Universities.
